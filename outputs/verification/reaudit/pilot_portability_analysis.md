## Exact numerical mechanism

The [focused Linux receipt](pilot_portability_reviewer_linux_x86_focus.json) identifies the two return entries. The [tail verifier](../../../verification/verify_pilot_tail_libm.py) reconstructs their exact PCG64 raw words and uniform draws from the manifest seeds and NumPy's third spawned exogenous component stream. It applies the [NumPy 2.3.5 Ziggurat tail formula](https://github.com/numpy/numpy/blob/v2.3.5/numpy/random/src/distributions/distributions.c) and its [published constants](https://github.com/numpy/numpy/blob/v2.3.5/numpy/random/src/distributions/ziggurat_constants.h). Both normal magnitudes exceed the 3.6541528853610088 tail cutoff. The same raw words and uniforms occur on both platforms; `log1p(-U)` rounds one ULP differently. The reconstructed normals and returns exactly match the published and Linux-regenerated values.

Exact raw words, stream indices and normal values are in the [macOS tail receipt](pilot_tail_libm_supported_macos.json) and [Linux tail receipt](pilot_tail_libm_reviewer_linux_x86.json). The discrepancy originates in platform `log1p` rounding on two rare normal tail draws, not changed RNG bits, seeds, behavior choices or fills. The price and cash recurrence rounds away these tiny return changes in the saved public arrays.

Posterior log weights also have last-bit cross-platform drift. In this reproduced Linux x86 build, stored-versus-refitted maxima are 3.637978807091713e-12 for both focus pilots, while fits on the original and regenerated Linux public arrays match exactly. The independent reviewer reported maxima of about 3.64e-12 and 7.28e-12. The second figure is not replicated here and can vary with another numerical build or accumulation path; all are below the predeclared 1e-9 fit limit, and winning models are unchanged. The [supported focus receipt](pilot_portability_supported_focus.json), [macOS x86 focus receipt](pilot_portability_x86_focus.json) and Linux full/focus receipts retain all nine weight deltas and episode likelihoods. Fit arithmetic drift is distinct from the two tail-normal public bytes.

## Verifier contract hardening

After the Linux numerical replay, an [isolated end-to-end fault injection](../../../verification/inject_pilot_portability_faults.py) exposed three gaps in that replay's checker version. The [pre-change receipt](pilot_portability_fault_injection_before.json) shows it accepted matching NaNs in `cash`, an altered stored-fit winner within the log-weight tolerance, and a configuration with reordered environments. It already rejected a NaN execution price on an executed side and a finite execution price on an unexecuted side. The checker now requires all float fields finite except execution prices, whose NaN mask must equal `executed_quantities == 0` in both arrays; original, stored and regenerated fit winners must agree; and the original configuration bytes, validated full protocol, ordered environments and protocol fingerprint must agree with the campaign manifest. The [post-change receipt](pilot_portability_fault_injection_after.json) rejects all five injected faults. Both receipts bind unchanged SHA-256s for the canonical `03_01` and `08_02` archives. A fresh hardened [supported all-90 receipt](pilot_portability_supported.json) passes 90/90; the two stored/original/regenerated winners are 3/3/3 and 8/8/8.

The Linux 88/90 receipt above was generated by the **pre-hardening** checker and was not rerun merely for checker-version uniformity. The hardened supported receipt identifies `checker_contract_version: 2` and its checker SHA-256; the older Linux receipt lacks those fields. Its raw numerical evidence remains the exact-stack result: all Linux fields except the two identified finite returns were byte-identical to canonical originals; its fit winner indices agree with the unchanged stored fits; and its manifest SHA matches the configuration authenticated by the hardened supported run. These facts cover the newly added gates for that recorded run, but they are an indirect reconciliation rather than a claim that the hardened checker executed on Linux.
